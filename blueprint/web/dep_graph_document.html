Corollary 27Garnett II.3.2: \(H^\infty \) boundary values, disc form

Source: Garnett, Chapter II, Section 3, PDF page 67. Every \(f\in H^\infty (\mathbb {D})\) has a nontangential limit at almost every boundary point, and its boundary function satisfies \(\| f^*\| _{L^\infty (d\theta /2\pi )}\le \| f\| _\infty \).

Proof ▶

The real and imaginary parts of \(f\) are bounded harmonic, hence Poisson integrals of \(L^\infty \) boundary data by Theorem 12; an \(L^\infty \) density is in particular an \(L^1\) density, so Theorem 11 gives nontangential limits almost everywhere. The norm bound needs no maximal function: \(\| f(z)\| \le \| f\| _\infty \) pointwise on \(\mathbb {D}\), and the bound passes to the limit along the filter. This is the only part of Garnett’s Corollary II.3.2 used here; the remaining \(H^p\) cases are not needed.

Corollary 19Garnett II.4.2: boundary uniqueness

Source: Garnett, Chapter II, Section 4, PDF page 75. Let \(f\in N\), and suppose the boundary function \(f^*\) vanishes on a subset of \(\partial \mathbb {D}\) of positive Lebesgue measure. Then \(f\equiv 0\). Since \(H^p\subseteq N\) for every \(p{\gt}0\), this contains Garnett’s statement for \(H^p(\mathbb {D})\), \(0{\lt}p\le \infty \).

Proof ▶

Contrapositive. If \(f\not\equiv 0\) then part (2) of Theorem 28 gives \(\log |f^*|\in L^1(d\theta /2\pi )\), so \(\log |f^*|{\gt}-\infty \) and hence \(f^*\ne 0\) almost everywhere. The passage from \(H^p\) to \(N\) is the inclusion hpDisc_le_nevanlinnaDisc, already formalized.

Garnett argues the other way round, deducing this from Theorem II.4.1, whose proof dominates \(\log ^+|f(re^{i\theta })|\) by \((1/p)|f^*|^p\) with \(f^*\) the nontangential maximal function of Theorem II.3.1. Reversing the implication keeps the present development free of the maximal function and of the Hardy–Littlewood theory behind it, at the cost of making this corollary depend on Theorem 28. There is no circularity: Theorem 28 obtains the nonvanishing of the boundary values of \(e^{-(u_j+iv_j)}\) from a second application of Theorem 11, not from this corollary. What is lost is Garnett’s sharper inequality II.4.1 itself, \(\log |f(z_0)|\le \int \log |f^*|P_{z_0}\, d\theta /2\pi \) for \(f\in H^p\), which is not needed for Theorem 36; it would be needed for the \(N^+\) refinements, and there the maximal function would have to be developed anyway.

Definition 4Boundary function

The boundary function of \(f\) is defined by the radial limit

\[ f^*(\zeta )=\lim _{r\uparrow 1}f(r\zeta ), \]

interpreted through the choice operator, so that \(f^*\) is a total function requiring no hypothesis on \(f\) or on \(\zeta \). Since \([0,1)\zeta \subset \Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\) for every \(\alpha {\gt}1\), whenever the nontangential limit at \(\zeta \) exists it is equal to \(f^*(\zeta )\). All statements below are therefore of the shape “\(f\) has a nontangential limit at almost every \(\zeta \), and its value is \(f^*(\zeta )\)”: the existence assertion carries the strength, while the value is a total, well-behaved function that can be fed to \(L^p\) statements without side conditions.

Definition 3Nontangential limit

Source: Garnett, Chapter I, Section 5, PDF page 27. The nontangential filter at \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\) is

\[ \mathcal{N}_\zeta =\sup _{\alpha {\gt}1}\ \mathcal{N}_{\Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )}(\zeta ), \]

the supremum, in the lattice of filters, of the neighbourhood filters of \(\zeta \) within \(\Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\), and \(\operatorname {nt}\lim _{z\to \zeta }f(z)=L\) means that \(f\) tends to \(L\) along \(\mathcal{N}_\zeta \). Two consequences of packaging the definition as a single filter: the limit is unique because \(\mathcal{N}_\zeta \) is nontrivial for \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\), and all the algebraic rules for nontangential limits (sums, products, scalar multiples, composition with continuous maps, squeezing) are inherited from the generic API for limits along filters, so none of them has to be proved.

Definition 2Nontangential approach region

Source: Garnett, Chapter I, Section 4, PDF page 23 (disc form). For \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\) and \(\alpha {\gt}1\) put

\[ \Gamma _\alpha (\zeta ) =\Bigl\{ z\in \mathbb {D}:\ |\zeta -z|{\lt}\alpha \, (1-|z|)\Bigr\} , \]

a teardrop-shaped region asymptotic at \(\zeta \) to an angle with vertex \(\zeta \). Facts to record: \(\Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\) is open and contained in \(\mathbb {D}\); it increases with \(\alpha \); it contains the radial segment \([0,1)\zeta \); it is empty when \(\alpha \le 1\); and \(\Gamma _\alpha (1)\) is mathlib’s Complex.stolzSet \(\alpha \), with \(\Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )=\zeta \cdot \Gamma _\alpha (1)\). Mathlib only has the region at \(\zeta =1\), so the rotated family has to be added.

Lemma 5Measurability of the boundary function

If \(f\) is continuous on \(\mathbb {D}\) and has nontangential limits almost everywhere on \(\partial \mathbb {D}\), then \(f^*\) is almost everywhere strongly measurable with respect to \(d\theta /2\pi \), being the almost everywhere pointwise limit of the continuous functions \(\zeta \mapsto f\bigl((1-\tfrac 1n)\zeta \bigr)\). This is the reason for defining \(f^*\) radially: a limit along a countably generated filter is a limit of a sequence, hence measurable.

Lemma 8Garnett I.5.4: singular Poisson integrals vanish at the boundary

Source: Garnett, Chapter I, Section 5, PDF pages 41–42. If \(\nu \) is a finite signed measure on \(\partial \mathbb {D}\) that is singular with respect to \(d\theta \), then

\[ \operatorname {nt}\lim _{z\to \zeta }P[\nu ](z)=0 \]

for almost every \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\). This is the step that removes the singular part in Theorem 11, and it is also what gives the singular functions of Definition 30 boundary modulus one almost everywhere.

Proof ▶

By the Jordan decomposition we may assume \(\nu \ge 0\). Fix \(\zeta \) at which \(\nu (I(\zeta ,h))/2h\to 0\), which by Theorem 7 is almost every \(\zeta \). For \(z\in \Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\) split the integral at \(|e^{i\theta }-\zeta |=A(1-|z|)\). On the far part Lemma 6 gives a bound \(C_\alpha \, \nu (\partial \mathbb {D})/(A^{2}(1-|z|))\), and on the near part the same lemma bounds the integral by \(C_\alpha \sup _{h{\lt}A(1-|z|)}\nu (I(\zeta ,h))/2h\). Choose \(A=A(z)\) with \(A(1-|z|)\to 0\) and \(A^{2}(1-|z|)\to \infty \) as \(|z|\to 1\).

Lemma 6Poisson kernel over a Stolz region

Let \(\alpha {\gt}1\). There is a constant \(C_\alpha \) such that for every \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\), every \(z\in \Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\) and every \(\theta \),

\[ P_z(\theta )\ \le \frac{C_\alpha \, (1-|z|)}{\bigl((1-|z|)+|e^{i\theta }-\zeta |\bigr)^{2}} . \]

In particular \(P_z(\theta )\le C_\alpha /(1-|z|)\) always, and \(P_z(\theta )\le C_\alpha (1-|z|)/|e^{i\theta }-\zeta |^{2}\) once \(|e^{i\theta }-\zeta |\ge 2\alpha (1-|z|)\). Together with \(\int P_z\, d\theta /2\pi =1\) this exhibits the Poisson kernels, restricted to a Stolz region, as an approximate identity with a uniformly integrable decreasing majorant. This single estimate replaces Garnett’s Theorem I.4.2 and is the engine of both Lemma 8 and Theorem 9.

Proof ▶

From \(|e^{i\theta }-z|\ge 1-|z|\) and, by the triangle inequality and the definition of \(\Gamma _\alpha (\zeta )\), \(|e^{i\theta }-z|\ge |e^{i\theta }-\zeta |-\alpha (1-|z|)\), one gets \(|e^{i\theta }-z|\ge c_\alpha \bigl((1-|z|)+|e^{i\theta }-\zeta |\bigr)\); insert this into \(P_z(\theta )=(1-|z|^2)/|e^{i\theta }-z|^{2}\) and use \(1-|z|^2\le 2(1-|z|)\).

Theorem 12Bounded harmonic functions are Poisson integrals of \(L^\infty \) data

If \(u\) is real harmonic on \(\mathbb {D}\) with \(|u|\le M\), then there is \(k\in L^\infty (d\theta /2\pi )\) with \(\| k\| _\infty \le M\) and \(u=P[k\, d\theta /2\pi ]\).

Proof ▶

Apply Theorem 25 to the positive harmonic function \(u+M\) to get \(u+M=P[\mu ]\) with \(\mu \ge 0\) finite. Since \(\mu \) is the weak-star limit of the measures \((u(r\, \cdot )+M)\, d\theta /2\pi \), whose densities are bounded by \(2M\), one gets \(\mu \le 2M\, d\theta /2\pi \); Radon–Nikodym then produces a density bounded by \(2M\), and subtracting \(M\) gives \(k\).

Theorem 7Differentiation of measures on the circle

Let \(\nu \) be a finite positive Borel measure on \(\partial \mathbb {D}\) and let \(I(\zeta ,h)\) denote the arc of length \(2h\) centred at \(\zeta \). Then for almost every \(\zeta \),

\[ \lim _{h\to 0}\frac{\nu \bigl(I(\zeta ,h)\bigr)}{2h} =\frac{d\nu }{d\theta }(\zeta ), \]

and in particular \(\nu (I(\zeta ,h))/2h\to 0\) almost everywhere when \(\nu \perp d\theta \). In the same setting, almost every \(\zeta \) is a Lebesgue point of a given \(k\in L^1(d\theta /2\pi )\):

\[ \lim _{h\to 0}\ \frac{1}{\mu \bigl(I(\zeta ,h)\bigr)} \int _{I(\zeta ,h)}|k-k(\zeta )|\, d\mu \ =\ 0, \qquad \mu =d\theta /2\pi . \]

Both halves come from mathlib applied directly on \(\mathbb {C}\) with \(\mu =\) circleMeasure 0 1, with no passage to intervals in \(\theta \): Besicovitch.ae_tendsto_rnDeriv needs only local finiteness of the two measures, Besicovitch.vitaliFamily needs only \(s\)-finiteness, and VitaliFamily.ae_tendsto_average_norm_sub then gives the Lebesgue points; HasBesicovitchCovering holds for every finite-dimensional real normed space, in particular for \(\mathbb {C}\). The singular case follows since the Radon–Nikodym derivative of a mutually singular measure vanishes almost everywhere.

The Vitali families of MeasureTheory.Covering.DensityTheorem are not usable here: they require IsUnifLocDoublingMeasure, which fails for circleMeasure viewed as a measure on \(\mathbb {C}\), since balls centred slightly off the circle have measure zero while their doubles do not; the Besicovitch family carries no such hypothesis. And \(\overline{B}(\zeta ,h)\cap \partial \mathbb {D}\) is the arc \(I(\zeta ,h)\), so the only genuine work is the comparability \(\mu \bigl(\overline B(\zeta ,h)\bigr)\asymp h\) of chord and arc, which Lemma 6 needs anyway. Theorem 14Garnett II.2.1: harmonic majorants imply the Blaschke condition

Source: Garnett, Chapter II, Section 2, PDF page 63. Let \(f\) be a nonzero analytic function on \(\mathbb {D}\), and let \(\{ a_n\} \) be its zeros, counted with multiplicity. If \(\log |f|\) has a harmonic majorant on \(\mathbb {D}\), then

\[ \sum _n (1-|a_n|){\lt}\infty . \]

Moreover, if \(f(0)\ne 0\) and \(u\) is the least harmonic majorant of \(\log |f|\), then

\[ \sum _n (1-|a_n|) \le u(0)-\log |f(0)|. \]

By Theorem 23, the harmonic-majorant hypothesis is equivalent to \(f\) belonging to the Nevanlinna class \(N\).

Proof ▶

After removing a possible zero at the origin, fix \(r{\lt}1\) avoiding the moduli of the zeros and divide \(f(rz)\) by the finite Blaschke product with the same zeros. The logarithm of the modulus of the resulting zero-free analytic function is harmonic, so the mean-value formula gives the familiar Jensen identity

\[ \log |f(0)|+\sum _{|a_j|{\lt}r}\log \frac{r}{|a_j|} =\frac{1}{2\pi }\int _0^{2\pi }\log |f(r e^{i\theta })|\, d\theta . \]

Theorem 1 identifies the limit of the right-hand side with \(u(0)\). Letting \(r\to 1\) and using \(1-|a_j|\le \log (1/|a_j|)\) proves both conclusions.

Theorem 28Garnett II.5.3: boundary values and the least-majorant measure

Source: Garnett, Chapter II, Section 5, PDF page 79. Let \(f\in N\), \(f\not\equiv 0\). Then:

  1. \(f\) has a nontangential limit \(f^*(\zeta )\) at almost every \(\zeta \in \partial \mathbb {D}\), and \(f^*\ne 0\) almost everywhere;

  2. \(\log |f^*|\in L^1(d\theta /2\pi )\);

  3. the least harmonic majorant of \(\log |f|\) is \(P[\mu ]\) for a unique finite signed measure \(\mu \) on \(\partial \mathbb {D}\), and

    \begin{equation} \label{eq:II_5_3_decomposition} d\mu =\log |f^*|\, \frac{d\theta }{2\pi }+d\mu _s, \qquad \mu _s\perp d\theta . \end{equation}
    1

In other words, the absolutely continuous part of the representing measure is exactly the boundary logarithm, and the whole obstruction to the inequality \(\log |f(z)|\le \int \log |f^*|P_z\, d\theta /2\pi \) is concentrated in the singular part; Garnett’s class \(N^+\) is the subclass where \(\mu _s\le 0\).

Proof ▶

Let \(B\) be the Blaschke product over the zeros of \(f\) and \(g=f/B\). By Theorem 23 and Lemma 26, \(g\) is zero-free and \(\log |g|=P[\mu ]\) is the least harmonic majorant of \(\log |f|\); by Theorem 13 the measure \(\mu \) is unique. Write \(d\mu =k\, d\theta /2\pi +d\mu _s\) using Theorem 10. By Theorem 11, \(\log |g|\) has nontangential limit \(k\) almost everywhere, and since \(|B^*|=1\) almost everywhere (Theorem 15) the same holds for \(\log |f|\). So \(k\in L^1\) and ?? follow once \(f\) itself is known to have nontangential limits.

For that, decompose \(\log |g|=u_1-u_2\) with \(u_j=P[\mu _j]\ge 0\) harmonic, \(\mu =\mu _1-\mu _2\) the Jordan decomposition. Choose harmonic conjugates \(v_j\) with \(v_j(0)=0\), which exist because the disc is simply connected and which mathlib provides as InnerProductSpace.HarmonicOnNhd.exists_analyticOnNhd_ball_re_eq; then \(\log g=(u_1+iv_1)-(u_2+iv_2)+ic\) for a real constant \(c\), so that

\[ g=e^{ic}\, \frac{e^{-(u_2+iv_2)}}{e^{-(u_1+iv_1)}} . \]

Each \(e^{-(u_j+iv_j)}\) has modulus \(e^{-u_j}\le 1\), hence lies in \(H^\infty \) and has nontangential limits almost everywhere by Corollary 27. Those limits are nonzero almost everywhere: \(\log |e^{-(u_j+iv_j)}|=-P[\mu _j]\) has a finite nontangential limit almost everywhere by Theorem 11 again, so the boundary modulus is \(e^{-k_j}{\gt}0\) almost everywhere. Hence \(g\), and then \(f=Bg\), have nontangential limits almost everywhere.

Garnett obtains the nonvanishing from the boundary uniqueness theorem (Corollary 19) instead. Using Theorem 11 as above keeps II.5.3 independent of the \(H^p\) boundary uniqueness theory, which is why cor:II_4_2 is not among the dependencies above; it is still needed for the outer factor.

Theorem 10Lebesgue decomposition of a finite boundary measure

A finite signed Borel measure \(\mu \) on \(\partial \mathbb {D}\) decomposes uniquely as

\[ d\mu =k\, \frac{d\theta }{2\pi }+d\mu _s, \qquad k\in L^1(d\theta /2\pi ),\quad \mu _s\perp d\theta . \]

This is available in mathlib: MeasureTheory.SignedMeasure.singularPart_add_withDensity_rnDeriv_eq, SignedMeasure.rnDeriv, SignedMeasure.integrable_rnDeriv, the signed density operator Measure.withDensity for vector measures, and the mutual-singularity relation VectorMeasure.MutuallySingular. Only the ambient space has to be fixed, which the convention at the head of this section does.

Theorem 34Singular measure determines singular factors

The positive and negative singular parts of the representing measure determine \(S_1\) and \(S_2\). Conversely, equality of the corresponding singular functions forces equality of the singular measures, modulo the normalization convention.

Theorem 32Zeros determine the Blaschke factor

The multiset of zeros in \(\mathbb {D}\) determines the associated Blaschke product up to the chosen unimodular normalization. With a fixed convention for elementary factors, the product is uniquely determined.
